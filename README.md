Deep eutectic solvents (DESs) are considered promising green alternatives to conventional organic solvents, but their melting temperature is difficult to predict due to complex intermolecular interactions. In this work, machine-learning methods were applied to predict the melting temperature of DES mixtures using a dataset of approximately 2300 samples obtained from the DESignSolvents database. Molecular structures were represented using SMILES strings, and additional molecular descriptors were generated with RDKit. Mixture-level descriptors were calculated as composition-weighted averages.
Several regression models were compared, including Linear Regression, Ridge Regression, Kernel Ridge Regression, and Random Forest. Model performance was evaluated using mean absolute error, root mean squared error, and the coefficient of determination (R²). Linear and Ridge regression models showed very poor performance, indicating that linear relationships are not sufficient to describe DES melting behavior. Kernel Ridge Regression significantly improved prediction accuracy, while Random Forest achieved the best results, with an R² of 0.85.
Feature-importance analysis revealed that structural descriptors related to hydrogen bonding, molecular size, and functional groups play a dominant role, while compositional effects were also relevant. Overall, this study demonstrates that nonlinear machine-learning models are effective tools for predicting DES melting temperatures and for gaining insight into structure–property relationships.
